About

An easel before an interface.

I'm a product designer studying at the University of Michigan School of Information, graduating this December. Right now I'm at SailPoint, designing an AI-assisted wizard that lets non-technical admins connect complex systems without writing a line of code.

What keeps pulling me back to this work is the problem-solving itself. I like sitting with something tangled and finding the one move that makes it click. And I like that I can actually build the thing, so an idea doesn't die as a nice thought in a doc. Taking a feature, or a whole product, and leaving it sharper than I found it is the part that never gets old.

To me, design is subtraction. Good UX simplifies. It doesn't pile on features or explanations until the noise drowns out the point. A lot of my work is figuring out what to take away, and my favorite kind of win is when the interface finally gets quiet and the thing just works.

Most of that happens with other people. My best ideas come out of bouncing something half-formed off another designer or an engineer and watching it get better in the exchange. I'd rather be in the room arguing toward a stronger answer than defending a finished one.

Right now I'm looking for a team building real AI product, not AI demos, where I can help people stay in control of the systems working on their behalf.
